> [!theorem] Theorem. (A)
> Let $a,b,c\in G$ where $G$ is a [[group]].
> 1. If $ab=ac$ then $b=c$
> 2. If $ba=ca$ then $b=c$

> [!proof] Proof. (A)
> 1. Suppose $ab=ac$. Then:
> 	$$
> 	> \begin{align}
> 	a^{-1}ab&=a^{-1}ac \\
> 	eb&=ec \\
> 	b&=c.
> 	\end{align}
>
> 	$$
> 2. Suppose $ba=ca$. Then:
> 	$$
> 	> \begin{align}
> 	baa^{-1}&=caa^{-1}  \\
> 	be&=ce \\
> 	b&=c.
> 	\end{align}
> 	$$

> [!remark]
> - we just appended $a^{-1}$ to both sides
> - $ab=ca\not\rightarrow b=c$, in general
> - a counter-example can be found in $GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})$
