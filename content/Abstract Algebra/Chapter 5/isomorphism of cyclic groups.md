- since [[Cyclic Subgroup|cyclic subgroups]] have one-to-one correspondence between $\mathbb{Z}_{n}$, we can state $f(i)=a^{i}$ is one-to-one from $\mathbb{Z}$ to $\left\langle a \right\rangle$ for some $G=\left\langle a \right\rangle$: $$f(i+j)=a^{i+j}=a^{i}a^{j}=f(i)f(j)$$
- $f$ is called an **isomorphism** from $\mathbb{Z}_{n}$ to $\left\langle a \right\rangle$. 
	- in other words, $\mathbb{Z}_{n}\tilde=\left\langle a \right\rangle$
we define the following theorem:

> [!theorem] Isomorphism of Cyclic Groups:
> 1. $\forall n>0$, every cyclic group of order $n$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_{n}$. So any two cyclic groups of order $n$ are *isomorphic* to each other
> 2. Every cyclic group of order infinity is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}$, so any two cyclic groups of order infinity are *isomorphic*
>
> If $G$ is any group and $a\in G$:
> 1. $a^{m}a^{n}=a^{m+n}$
> 2. $(a^{n})^{-1}=a^{-n}$
> 3. The set of all the powers of $a$ is a subgroup of $G$.
