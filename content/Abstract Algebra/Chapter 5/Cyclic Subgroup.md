> [!definition]
> Let $G$ be a [[Groups|group]]. Let $a\in G$. The **cyclic subgroup** generated by $a$ is $\langle a \rangle=\left\{ a^{n}|n\in\mathbb{Z} \right\}=\left\{ ..., a^{-2},a^{-1}, a^{0}, a^{1}, a^{2}, ... \right\}$
> - note, this is the *smallest* [[subgroup]] of $G$ containing $a$.
> - note, if $a^{n}=e$ for some $n$ then $\langle a \rangle=\left\{ e,a,a^{2},...,a^{n-1} \right\}$
> - conversely, if $\langle a \rangle$ is finite, then $a^{j}=a^{k}$ for some integers $j\neq k$.
> 	~~~ad-proof
> 	WLOG, assume $j<k$. Then $a^{j}a^{-j}=a^{k}a^{-j}$, so $e=a^{k-j}$. Thus $a^{n}=e$ where $n=k-j\in \mathbb{Z}^{+}$ for some $n\in \mathbb{Z}^{+}$

- most commonly expressed in [[additive cyclic subgroup|additive notation]] or [[multiplicative cyclic subgroup|multiplicative notation]]

> [!theorem]
> Every cyclic group $G$ is [[abelian]] if $G=\langle a \rangle$ for some $a\in G$

> [!proof]
> $G=\langle a \rangle$ for some $a\in G$ i.e $G=\left\{ a^{n}\ |\ n\in \mathbb{Z} \right\}$:
> Consider two arbitrary elements of $G$ which must have the form $a^{j},\ a^{k}$ for some $j,k\in \mathbb{Z},\ a^{j}a^{k}=a^{j+k}=a^{k+j}=a^{k}a^{j}$

> [!remark]
> Not every abelian group is cyclic!
> - e.g. $\mathbb{Q},\ \mathbb{R},\ \mathbb{Z} \times\mathbb{Z}$

> [!theorem]
> If $G$ is a cyclic group and $H$ is a subgroup of $G$, then $H$ is cyclic.

> [!proof]
> Where $G=\mathbb{Z}$:
> Let $H$ be a subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}$.
> ##### Case 1: $H=0$
>    Then $H=\langle 0 \rangle$
> ##### Case 2: $H\neq 0$
>    Then there is some non-zero $a\in H$
> WLOG assume $a>0$ since $H$ is closed under negatives. By the Well-Ordering property of $\mathbb{Z}^{+}$, the non-empty set $H\cap\mathbb{Z}^{+}$ has a *smallest* element. Let $b$ be the smallest element.
>    *Claim*: $H=\left\langle b \right\rangle$
> 	$\left\langle b \right\rangle \subset H$ since $b\in H$ and $H$ is a subgroup
> Suppose toward a contradiction there is some $c\in H$ but $c\not\in \left\langle b \right\rangle$. Then $nb<c<(n+1)b$ for some $n\in \mathbb{Z}$. Since $c, nb\in H,\ c-nb\in H$. Then $0<c-nb<b$. Contradiction: $b$ is the smallest positive element of $H$.

> Proof, that $\langle a \rangle$ is a subgroup:
> 1. (closure) Let $a^{n}, a^{m}\in \langle a \rangle$. Then $a^{n}a^{m}=a^{n+m}\in \langle a \rangle$
> 2. (identity) Let $a^{n}\in \langle a \rangle$. Then $(a^{n})^{-1}=a^{n}\in\langle a \rangle$

- oftentimes cyclic subgroups can be proven using induction:

> [!example]
> if $a,b\in G$ where $G$ is abelian and $n\in \mathbb{Z}$ then $(ab)^{n}=a^{n}b^{n}$
> **basis**: suppose $n=0$
> 	then $(ab)^{0}=e$ and $a^{0}b^{0}=ee=e$ also
> **induction**: suppose $(ab)^{n}=a^{n}b^{n}$. WTS $(ab)^{n+1}=a^{n+1}b^{n+1}$
> 	$(ab)^{n+1}=(ab)^{n}ab=a^{n}b^{n}ab=a^{n}ab^{n}b=a^{n+1}b^{n+1}$
>
> negatives: $-n$ where $-n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}$, WTS $(ab)^{-n}=a^{-n}b^{-n}$
> **basis**: suppose $n=1$.
> 	then $(ab)^{-1}=b^{-1}a^{-1}=a^{-1}b^{-1}$
> **induction**: suppose $n\geq 0$ and $(ab)^{-n}=a^{-n}b^{-n}$. WTS $(ab)^{-(n+1)}=a^{-(n+1)}b^{-(n+1)}$
> 	then
> 	$$
> 	> \begin{align}
> 	(ab)^{-(n+1)}&=(ab)^{-n-1} \\
> 	&=(ab)^{-n}(ab)^{-1} \\
> 	&=a^{-n}b^{-n}(ab)^{-1} \\
> 	&=a^{-n}b^{-n}b^{-1}a^{-1} \\
> 	&=a^{-n}a^{-1}b^{-n}b^{-1} \\
> 	&=a^{-n-1}b^{-n-1} \\
> 	&=a^{-(n+1)}b^{-(n+1)}.
> 	\end{align}
>
> 	$$

#### Proof that $\mathbb{Z} \times\mathbb{Z}$ is not cyclic

> [!example]
> Suppose toward a contradiction $\mathbb{Z} \times\mathbb{Z}=\langle (a,b) \rangle$ for some $(a,b)\in \mathbb{Z} \times\mathbb{Z}$.
> ##### Case 1: $(a,b)$
> - in quadrant $I$ or $III$
> 	- i.e. $a,b$ have same sign or at least one is $0$
> - then $(-1,1)\not\in\langle a,b \rangle$ - check
> ##### Case 2: $(a,b)\text{ in quadrant }II, IV$
> - then $(1,1)\not\in\langle a,b \rangle$, contradiction

> [!remark]
> Is $\mathbb{Z}_{2} \times\mathbb{Z}_{3}$ cyclic?
> $\mathbb{Z}_{2} \times\mathbb{Z}_{3}=\left\{ (0,0),(0,1),(0,2),(1,0),(1,1),(1,2) \right\}$
> $\langle (0,0) \rangle=\left\{ (0,0)\right\}$
> $\langle (0,1) \rangle= \left\{ (0,0),(0,1),(0,2) \right\}$
> $\langle (0,2) \rangle= \left\{ (0,0), (0,2),(0,1) \right\}$
> $\langle (1,0) \rangle=\left\{ (0,0),(1,0) \right\}$
> $\langle (1,1) \rangle= \left\{ (0,0),(1,1),(0,2),(1,0),(0,1),(1,2) \right\} =\mathbb{Z}_{2} \times\mathbb{Z}_{3}$
> $= \langle (1,2) \rangle$
> - $\mathbb{Z}_{2} \times\mathbb{Z}_{4}$ is not cyclic:
>
> $\langle (1,1)\rangle =\left\{ (0,0),(1,1),(0,2)(1,3) \right\}\neq \mathbb{Z}_{2}  \times \mathbb{Z_{4}}$
> - You can show that no other element generates it either and so their least common multiple is *less than* $8$. This has to do w/ fact that $2, 4$ have a common divisor ($2$)

> [!remark]
> We can use: 
> $$
> > \begin{align}
> A^{3}&=I \\
> B^{2}&=I \\
> BA&=A^{2}B
> \end{align}
>
> $$
>  ... to fill in a table for $\left\langle A, B \right\rangle=\left\{ I,A,A^{2}, B, AB, A^2B \right\}$ using only these relations. This approach is called *defining a group by generators and relations*.
>  Ex.
>  Make a table for $\left\{ e,a,b,ab \right\}$ where $a^{2}=e,\ b^{2}=e,\ ab=ba$
>  $$
> \left(\begin{array}{c|cccc}
>    & \text{e} & \text{a} & \text{b} & \text{ab} \\
> \text{e} & e & a & b & ab \\
> \text{a} & a & e & ab & b \\
> \text{b} & b & ab & e & a \\
> \text{ab} & ab & b & a & e
> \end{array}\right)
> aab=b
> $$

#### Pf. Any subgroup of a cyclic group is a subgroup

> [!proof]
> Let $m$ be the smallest positive integer such that $a^{m}\in H$. We will show that every element of $H$ is a power of $a^{m}$, hence $a^{m}$ is a generator of $H$.
> Let $a^{t}$ be *any* element of $H$. Divide $t$ by $m$ using the division algorithm:
> $$
> > \begin{align}
> t&=mq+r&&0\leq r<m  \\
> a^{t}&=a^{mq+r} \\
> &=a^{mq}a^{r} \\
> a^{r}&=(a^{mq})^{-1}a^{t} \\
> &=(a^{m})^{-q}a^{t}
> \end{align}
>
> $$
> ... but $a^{m}\in H$ and $a^{t}\in H$; thus $(a^{m})^{-q}a^{t}\in H$.
