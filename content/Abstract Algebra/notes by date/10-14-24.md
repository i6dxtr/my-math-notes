> [!definition]
> The *order of a group* is equal to its **cardinality** i.e. the number of elements

- order of a group element $a_{1}$ is equal to the order of $\left\langle a \right\rangle$

> [!theorem]
> Every group of order 4 is isomorphic to either $\mathbb{Z}_{4}$ or $\mathbb{Z}_{2} \times \mathbb{Z}_{2}$
> - latter case is called a *Klein group*

> [!proof]
> Let $G=\left\{ e,a.b.c \right\}$
> - *Case 1*: $G$ is cyclic. Then $G\cong \mathbb{Z}_{4}$
> - *Case 2*: $G$ is not cyclic. WTS $G\cong \mathbb{Z}_{2} \times \mathbb{Z}_{2}$
> 	- We use the following theorem:
> 		- If $G$ is a group & $a\in G$, then $\text{ord}(G)=k\cdot\text{ord}(a)\ \exists\ k\in \mathbb{Z}$
> 			- $\text{ord}(G)$ divisible by $\text{ord}(a)$
> 	- so its necessary that $1\in G$ or $2\in G$
> 		- not $4$ since $G$ isn't cyclic
> 	- So $a^{2}=e$, $b^{2}=e$, and $c^{2}=e$
> 		- construct table:
> 			$$
> 			\left(\begin{array}{c|cccc}
> 			&\textbf{e}&\textbf{a}&\textbf{b}&\textbf{c} \\
> 			\textbf{e} &e&a&b&c \\
> 			\textbf{a} &a&e&c&b \\
> 			\textbf{b} &b&c&e&a \\
> 			\textbf{c} &c&b&a&e \\
> 			\end{array}\right)
> 			$$
> 			... $ab=c$ since it can't be the same as anything else in its row or column
> 		- also construct binary table ($\mathbb{Z}_{2} \times \mathbb{Z}_{2}$)

==recall==: if $f:G_{1}\rightarrow G_{2}$ is a homomorphism, $a\in G_{1}$ and $n\in\mathbb{Z}$, then $f(a^{n})=f(a)^{n}$

> [!theorem]
> If $f:G_{1}\rightarrow G_{2}$ is an isomorphism and $a\in G_{1}$, then $\text{ord}(f(a))=\text{ord}(a)$

> [!corollary]
> If $G_{1}\cong G_{2}$ & $\exists\ a\in G_{1}$ s.t. $\text{ord}(a)=n$ then $\exists\ b\in G_{2}$ s.t. $\text{ord}(b)=n$

### Cayley's Theorem

> [!theorem]
> Every group is isomorphic to a group of permutations (i.e., to a subgroup of $S_{A}$ for some $A$)

> [!proof]
> Showing $G$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $S_{G}$:
> $\forall a\in G$, define $\Pi _{a}\in S_{G}$ by $\Pi_{a}(x)=ax$ 
> - $\Pi_{a}$ is left-multiplication by $a$
> *Claim*: $\Pi_{a}$ is actually in $S_{G}$
> *Pf.* $\Pi_{a}$ is a function from $G\rightarrow G$:
> - Since $\forall x\in G\ \ ax\in G$, $\Pi_{a}$ is a bijection because it has an inverse function $\Pi_{a^{-1}}:$ 
> 	- $\Pi_{a}(\Pi_{a^{-1}}(x))=\Pi_{a}(a^{-1}x)=aa^{-1}x=x$
> 	- $\Pi_{a^{-1}}(\Pi_{a}(x))=\Pi_{a^{-1}}(ax)=a^{-1}ax=x$
> - So $\Pi_{a}$ is a permutation of $G$.
> Define $f:G\rightarrow S_{G}$ by $f(a)=\Pi_{a}$
> - recall $\Pi_{a}(x)=ax$
> *Claim*. $f$ is a homomorphism
> *Pf.* Let $a,b\in G$:
> - WTS $f(ab)=f(a)\circ f(b)$
> 	- meaning $\Pi_{ab}=\Pi_{a}\circ \Pi_{b}$
> - $\forall x\in G,\ \Pi_{ab}(x)=abx$ and $(\Pi_{a}\circ\Pi_{b})(x)=\Pi_{a}(\Pi_{b}(x))=\Pi_{a}(bx)=abx$
> *Claim*. $f$ is injective
> *Pf.* Let $a,b\in G$:
> - Suppose $f(a)=f(b)$
> - WTS $a=b$
> - $\Pi_{a}=\Pi_{b}$, so $\Pi_{a}(e)=\Pi_{b}(e)$, meaning $ae=be\longrightarrow a=b$
> - Define $H=\left\{ \Pi_{a}\ |\ a\in G \right\}=\text{range}(f)=\text{lm}(f)$
> - Since $f$ is a homomorphism, its range $H$ is a subgroup of $S_{G}$
> - So $f$ is an injective and surjective homomorphism onto $H$, i.e. an *isomorphism* $G\rightarrow H$
> 	- then $H$ is a group of permutations

> [!corollary] Lemma.
> Let $G$ be cyclic.
> 1. If $\text{ord}(G)=n$ then $G\cong \mathbb{Z}_{n}$
> 	1. ==Pf.== Let $a$ be a generator of $G$. So $\text{ord}(a)=n$ & $G=\left\{ e,a,a^{2},...,a^{n-1} \right\}$. Define $f:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\rightarrow G$ by $f(k)=a^{k}$ which is a bijection. WTS $f$ is a homomorphism: $f(i+_{n}j)=a^{i+j\text{ mod }n}=a^{i+j}$ (since $\text{ord}(a)=n$) $=a^{i}a^{j}=f(i)f(j)$
> 2. If $\text{ord}(G)=\infty$ then $G\cong\mathbb{Z}$
