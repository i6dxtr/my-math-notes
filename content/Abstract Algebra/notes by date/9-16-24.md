- $G=\left\{ e,a,a^{2},b,ab,a^{2}b \right\}$ where:
	- $a^{3}=e$

	- $ba=a^{2}b$

#### Going from Cayley graph to group table
- ..
- 2 kinds of edges
	- orange $\rightarrow$ multiplication by $a$
	- blue $\rightarrow$ multiplication by $b$
creation:
- pick a vertex to call $e$ and fill in names for other elements in terms of the generators (exc. pic 2)--$ab (\leftarrow$ or $ba$, so in this group $ab=ba$)
- then make the table:
	$$
	\begin{align}
	&\left(\begin{array}{c|cccc}
	& \underline{e} & \underline{a}  & \underline{b} & \underline{ab} \\
	\underline{e} &  e & a & b & ab\\
	\underline{a} &  a & e & ab & b \\
	\underline{b} & b &ab &  e & a \\
	\underline{ab} & ab & b & a & e
	\end{array}\right)\\
	&aab=b \\
	\end{align}
	$$
second condition for a graph to be a Cayley graph of a group:
- If 2 different paths starting at $x$ lead to the same destination, then the corresponding paths starting at any other vertex $y$ also lead to the same destination as each other
	- ex. let $x=e,\ y=a$. Starting from $x=e$ the $ba$ (red) path and the $aab$ (green) path lead to the same destination, namely $a^{2}b$

> ==Recall==: for functions $f:A\rightarrow B$, $g:B\rightarrow C$:
> - ...
> The composition is $(g o f)(x)=g(f(x))\ \ \forall x\in A$
> - If $A=B=C$ i.e. if we are just considering functions from $A$ to $A$, any 2 of them can be composed, so *composition* is an *operation* on $\left\{ f\ |\ f:A\rightarrow A \right\}$
> properties of the composition operation:
> 1. it is *not* commutative (unless $A<3$ elements)
> 	1. $f,g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}$    $f(x)=2x$    $g(x)=x+1$
> 	 $(f o g)(x)=f(g(x))=2(x+1)=2x+2$
> 	 $gof(x)=g(f(x))=g(2x)=2x+1$
> 1. it *is* associative
