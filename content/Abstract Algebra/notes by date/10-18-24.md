## Chapter 13
### Lagrange's theorem

> [!definition]
> If $G$ is a group and $H$ is a subgroup of $G$, then the order of $G$ is a multiple of the order of $H$.

> [!example]
> *e.g.* $S_{4}$ has order $24$
>    $D_{4}$ has order $8$, a multiple of $24$
>    $A_{4}$ has order $12$, a multiple of $24$
> *e.g.* $\mathbb{Z}_{6}$ has order $6$
>    $\left\{ 0,2,4 \right\}=\left\langle 2 \right\rangle$
>    $\left\{ 0,3 \right\}=\left\langle 3 \right\rangle$

> [!definition]
> Let $G$ be a group and $H$ a subgroup of $G$. Let $a\in G$. The set $Ha=\left\{ xa\ |\ x\in H \right\}$ is called a **right coset** of $a$. Moreover, $aH=\left\{ ax\ |\ x\in H \right\}$ is called a **left coset** of $a$.

> [!example]
> *e.g.* $G=S_{3}$, $H=\left\langle 1,2 \right\rangle=\left\{ e, (12) \right\}$, $a=(1\ 2\ 3)$, so $aH$ is a right coset and $Ha$ is a left coset.
>    $Ha=\left\{ e(1\ 2\ 3), (12)(123) \right\}=\left\{ (1\ 2\ 3), (2\ 3) \right\}$
>    $aH=\left\{ (1\ 2\ 3)e, (1\ 2\ 3)(1\ 2) \right\}=\left\{ (1\ 2\ 3),(1\ 3) \right\}$
> *e.g.* $G=\mathbb{Z}_{6}$, $H=\left\langle 3 \right\rangle=\left\{ 0, 3 \right\}$
>    $H+0=\left\{ 0+0, 3+0 \right\}=\left\{ 0, 3 \right\}$
>    $H+1=\left\{ 0+1, 3+1 \right\}=\left\{ 1, 4 \right\}$
>    $H+2=\left\{ \cdots \right\}=\left\{ 2,5 \right\}$
>    $H+3=\left\{ \cdots \right\}=\left\{ 3,0 \right\}=H+0$
>    $H+4=\left\{ \cdots \right\}=\left\{ 4,1 \right\}=H+1$
>    $H+5=\left\{ \cdots \right\}=\left\{ 5,2 \right\}=H+2$
> Note there are only 3 different cosets, and they form a partition of $G$.

> [!corollary] Lemma 1.
> The right cosets of $H$ form a partition of $G$ such that: 
> 1. They are pairwise disjoint: $$Ha\neq Hb\longrightarrow Ha\cap Hb=\emptyset$$
> 2. Their union is all of $G$: 
>          $\rightarrow$ Every element of $G$ is in some right coset $Ha$

> [!proof] If $Ha\cap Hb\neq \emptyset$ then $Ha=Hb$.
> *pf.* Suppose $\exists c\in Ha\cap Hb$. So $c=xa,\exists x\in H$ and $c=yb,\exists y\in H$. So $xa=yb$ and $a=x^{-1}yb$.
> *Claim.* $Ha=Hb$
> 	*Claim.* $Ha\subset Hb$
> 		*Proof.* Let $d\in Ha$, so $d=za=zx^{-1}yb$, with $zx^{-1}yb\in H$
> 	*Claim.* $Hb\subset Ha$
> 		*Proof.* Let $d\in Hb$, so $d=zb=zy^{-1}xa\in Ha$ where $zy^{-1}xa\in H$
