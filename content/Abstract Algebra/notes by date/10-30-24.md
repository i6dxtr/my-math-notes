1. $f(e_1e_{1})=f(e_{1})f(e_{1})$
2. $f(e_{1})=f(e_{1})f(e_{1})$

4. $e_{2}=f(e_{1})$
#### 8.2
Suppose $f$ is injective.
1. Showing $\text{ker}\left( f \right)=\left\{ e_{1} \right\}$
	1. $e_{1}\in\text{ker}\left( f \right)$ (by part 1)
2. Let $a\in\text{ker}\left( f \right)$
3. Showing $a=e$
	1. $f(a)=e_{2}=f(e_{1})$ (by part 1)
4. So $a=e$
Conversely:
1. Suppose $\text{ker}\left( f \right)=\left\{ e_{1} \right\}$
2. Showing $f$ is injective
	1. Let $a,b\in G_{1}$ and $f(a)=f(b)$
	2. Algebra:
		1. $f(a)f(a)^{-1}=f\left( b \right)f\left( a \right)^{-1}$
		2. $e_{2}=f\left( ba^{-1} \right)$
	3. So $ba^{-1}=e_{1}$
	4. So $ba^{-1}a=e_{1}a$
	5. thus $b=a$.

### Problem 9
Suppose $G$ is abelian
Suppose there exists a homomorphism $f:G\rightarrow H$ which is onto.
1. Showing $H$ is abelian: $\left( ab=ba \right)$
	1. Let $a,b\in H$
	2. $f$ is onto:
		1. $a=f\left( x \right)$ 
		2. $b=f\left(y \right)$
	3. $G$ is abelian:
		1. $xy=yx$
		2. $f(xy)=f(yx)$
	4. So $f(x)f(y)=f(y)f(x)$
	5. So $ab=ba$.
