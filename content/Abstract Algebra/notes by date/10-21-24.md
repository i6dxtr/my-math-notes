### ch. 13 cont.
We showed if $Ha\ne Hb$ then $Ha\cap Hb=\emptyset$
*Claim.* $\left\{ Ha\ |\ a\in G \right\}$ is a partition of $G$
*Proof*. The union of all right cosets is $G$:
$$
\begin{align}
\cup_{a\in G}Ha=G \begin{cases} \subset\text{ is clear since}Ha\text{ is a subset of }G \\ \subset : \text{Let }b\in  G. \text{Then }b=eb\in  Hb\text{, so }b\in  LHS \end{cases}
\end{align}
$$
*Claim.* All cosets have the same # of elements (as $H$, so each other)
*Proof.* Let $a\in G$. We show $\lvert H \rvert=\lvert Ha \rvert$.
1. Consider the function $f:H\rightarrow Ha$ defined by $f(x)=x$.
2. $f$ is surjective by definition of $Ha$.
3. We show $f$ is injective:
	1. Suppose $f(x_{1})=f(x_{2})$
	2. Then $x_{1}a=x_{2}a$
	3. $\rightarrow x_{1}aa^{-1}=x_{2}aa^{-1}$ 
	4. so $x_{1}=x_{2}$ ... log cancellation
4. $f$ is bijective
5. Thus, $\lvert H \rvert=\lvert Ha \rvert$. **Q.E.D**

we proved $\text{ord}(H)*\lvert Ha \rvert\in G=\text{ord}(G)$, equivalent to the following:

> [!definition]
> The **index** of $H$ in $G$ is $(G:H)$, which is equal to the number of right cosets of $H$ in $G$. Meaning,
> 1. $\lvert H \rvert\cdot(G:H)=\lvert G \rvert$
> 2. $(G:H)=\frac{\lvert G \rvert}{\lvert H \rvert}$, if $G,H$ are finite sets.
> *e.g.* The index of $\left\langle 3 \right\rangle\in \mathbb{Z}$
> 	$\left\langle 3 \right\rangle+0=\left\{ -3,0,3,6,... \right\}$
> 	$\left\langle 3 \right\rangle+1=\left\{ ...-2,1,4,7,... \right\}$
> 	$\left\langle 3 \right\rangle+2=\left\{ ...,-1,2,5,8,... \right\}$
> 	... forming a partition of $G$.

> [!corollary] Corollaries of Lagrange's Theorem
> 1. If $G$ is a group and $a\in G$, then the order of $a$ divides the order of $G$. ($\text{ord}(a)=\text{ord}(\left\langle a \right\rangle)$)
> 2. Every group of prime order is cyclic
> 	1. *pf.* Suppose the order of $G$ is a prime $p$. $\exists a\in G, a\neq e$ s.t.
> 		1. $\left\langle a \right\rangle$ is a subgroup of $G$ 
> 		2. $\lvert \left\langle a \right\rangle \rvert>1$ and order dividing $p$
> 		3. so $\lvert \left\langle a \right\rangle \rvert=p$
> 		4. so $\left\langle a \right\rangle=G$
> 3. Every group of prime order is abelian.
> 4. *Fermat's little theorem*: 
> 	1. Let $p$ be prime and $0<a<p$.
> 	2. Then $a^{p-1}\text{ mod }p=1$
> 	*pf.* Let $a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}$, the group of nonzero elements of $\mathbb{Z}_{p}$ under multiplication $\text{mod }p$.
> 		   1. Let $k=\text{ord}(a)$. 
> 		   2. By Lagrange's theorem, $k$ divides $p-1$, so let $p-1=nk$.
> 		   3. So $a^{p-1}=(a^{k})^{n}=e^{n}=e=1$ in $\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}$
> 		   4. So $a^{p-1}\text{ mod }p=1$

## Chapter 15: Quotient Groups
If $G$ is a group and $H$ is a subgroup, $$(G:H)=\frac{\lvert G \rvert}{\lvert H \rvert}=\lvert G/ H \rvert.$$
1. we will define $G/H$ ("G mod H") in a way to make the above statement true
2. $G / H$ will only be a *group* when $H$ is a *normal* subgroup
#### Defining the set
$$G / H=\left\{ Ha\ |\ a\in  G \right\}$$... or, the set of all right cosets.
==recall==. A subgroup $H$ of $G$ is *normal* if $\forall a\in G, \forall x\in H,\ axa^{-1}\in H$, meaning it's closed under conjugates.
==note==. if $H$ is normal, then $Ha=aH$ for all $a\in G$, meaning left and right cosets are the same
	*pf.* We show $aH\subset Ha$ and $Ha\subset aH$.
		1. $Ha\subset aH$: Let $h_{1}a\in Ha$ ($h_{1}\in H$)
		2. Then $h_{1}a=a(a^{-1}h_{1}a)\in aH$, since $a^{-1}h_{1}a\in H$
		3. WLOG $aH\subset Ha$ is similar

> [!definition]
> If $H$ is a normal subgroup of $G$, we define an operation on $G/H$ by, $$(Ha)(Hb)=H(ab).$$

> [!remark]
> We can show the above equation is *well-defined*
> Define the set: $$G/H=\left\{ Ha\ |\ a\in  G \right\}$$... as the set of all right cosets.
> *pf.* Suppose $Ha=Ha'$ and $Hb=Hb'$. By contradiction:
> 1. We show $H(ab)=H(a'b')$. 
> 	1. We show $H(ab)\subset H(a'b')$. 
> 		1. Let $h_{1}ab\in Hab_{1}$, since $h_{1}a\in Ha=Ha'$
> 		2. So $h_{1}a=h_{2}a'$
> 		3. So $h_{1}ab=h_{2}a'=a'h_{3}b$ where $h_{3}\in H$
> 		4. Since $h_{3}b\in Hb=Hb'$, we see $h_{3}b=h_{4}b'$
> 		5. $h_{4}b'=b'h_{5}$ where $h_{4}, h_{5}\in H$
> 		6. Thus, $h_{1}ab=h_{2}a'b=a'h_{3}b=a'h_{4}b'=h_{5}a'b'\in Ha'b'$ where $h_{5}\in H$.

> [!corollary] Lemma.
> If $H$ is normal, $h\in H$, and $c\in G$, then $hc=ch'$ for some $h'\in H$ (since $Hc=cH$).

An example of a operation not well defined:
*e.g.* $*$ on $\mathbb{Q}$ by $\frac{a}{b}*\frac{c}{d}=\frac{a+c}{b+d}$
- $\frac{1}{2}* \frac{1}{3}=\frac{1+1}{2+3}=\frac{2}{5}$
- $\frac{2}{4}* \frac{3}{9}=\frac{2+3}{4+9}= \frac{5}{13}$
