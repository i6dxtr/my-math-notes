> [!theorem]
> Let $f:G\rightarrow H$ be a [[homomorphism]] with [[kernel]] $K$. 
> Then, for all $a,b\in G$: $$f( a )=f( b )\Longleftrightarrow Ka=Kb$$

> [!proof]
> 1. Fix $a,b\in G$
> 2. Then $f( a )=f( b )\Longleftrightarrow f( a )f( b )^{-1}=e$
> 	1. $\Longleftrightarrow f( ab^{-1} )=e$
> 	2. $\Longleftrightarrow ab^{-1}\in K$
> 	3. $\Longleftrightarrow a\in Kb$
> 	4. $\Longleftrightarrow Ka=Kb$

> [!theorem]
> If $H$ is a [[Subgroups|subgroup]] of $G$, then $H$ is [[normal subgroup|normal]] *if and only if* $H$ is the [[kernel]] of some [[homomorphism]] from $G$ to another group. $$f( x )=Hx\longrightarrow f:G\rightarrow G / H$$

> [!theorem]
> If $H,K$ are [[normal subgroup|normal subgroups]] of $G$, then so is $H\cap K$.
> 1. Since $H$ is normal, there is a homomorphism$\Phi_{1}:G\rightarrow G_{1}$ with kernel $H$.
> 2. Since $K$ is normal, there is a homomorphism $\Phi_{2}: G\rightarrow G_{2}$ with kernel $K$.
> 3. Thus, there is a homomorphism $\Phi_{3}:G\rightarrow G_{1} \times G_{2}$ such that $\text{ker}( \Phi_{3} )=K\cap H$
> 	1. thus $\Phi_{3}$ defined as $\Phi_{3}( g )=( \Phi_{1}( g ),\Phi_{2}( g ) )$
> 	2. so $\text{ker}( \Phi_{3} )=\left( \text{ker}( \Phi_{1} )\cap \text{ker}( \Phi_{2} ) \right)$
> 		1. $=K\cap H.$

Given a group $G$, an *automorphism* of $G$ is an isomorphism of $G$ with itself. 
1. We write $\text{Aut}( G )$ for the set (group) of automorphisms of $G$.
2. Note that the operation is composition
3. Take $( \mathbb{R},+ )$
	1. an automorphism is $f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ where $f( x )=ax\begin{cases} a\neq 0\\a\in \mathbb{R} \\ \end{cases}$
4. An inner automorphism of $G$ is a function of the form $P_{a}( x )=axa^{-1}$ for some $a\in G$
	1. Homomorphsim: $P_{a}( x_{4} )=axya^{-1}=P_{a}( x )P_{a}( y )$
		1. s.p. $P_{a}( x )=P_{a}( y )$

		2. Given $z$, we want $x$ s.t. $P_{a}( x )=z$

	1. $I( G )$ is normal in $\text{Aut}( G )$
