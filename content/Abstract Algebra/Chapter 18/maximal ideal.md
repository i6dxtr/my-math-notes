> [!definition]
> Let $A$ be a ring and $I$ an [[ideal]] of $A$. We say $I$ is **maximal** if $I\not\subseteq A$ (but $I\subset A$, so a *proper* subset), but there is no ideal $J$ of $A$ with $I\not\subseteq J\not\subseteq A$.

> [!remark]
> The trivial ideal, $\left\{ 0 \right\}$, is maximal *if and only if* $A$ is a [[Fields|field]].

> [!proof]
> 1. Let $A$ be a commutative ring with unity.
> 2. Suppose $\left\{ 0 \right\}$ is maximal.
> 3. We'll show $A$ is a field i.e. all nonzero elements have inverses.
> 	1. Consider principal ideal $\left\langle a \right\rangle=\left\{ xa\ |\ x\in A \right\}$ and $\left\{ 0 \right\}\subset \left\langle a \right\rangle\subseteq A$
> 	2. Since $\left\{ 0 \right\}$ is maximal, $\left\langle a \right\rangle=A$
> 	3. So $1\in \left\langle a \right\rangle$, meaning $1=xa$ for some $x\in A$
> 	4. Therefore $x$ is an inverse for $a$ in $A$.
> 4. Conversely, you can show if $A$ is a field, then $\left\{ 0 \right\}$ is maximal.
