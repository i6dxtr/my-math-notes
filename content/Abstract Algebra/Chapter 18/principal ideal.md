### Principal Ideal
$$\forall x\in  A\ \exists a\in  A\text{ s.t. }\left\langle a \right\rangle=xa $$
- let $A$ be a commutative ring with unity
- the simplest example of an ideal is the set of all multiples of fixed element $a$ by all elements in the ring
	- i.e., the set of all products $xa$ as $a$ remains fixed
	- an ideal b/c:
		- $xa+ya=( x+y )a$
		- $-( xa )=( -x )a$
		- $y( xa )=( yx )a$
	- this is called the **principal ideal** generated by $a$
	- symbolized as $\left\langle a \right\rangle$

##### Lecture Definition

> [!definition]
> Let $A$ be a commutative ring and $a\in A$. The **principal [[ideal]]** generated by $a$ is: $$\left\langle a \right\rangle=\left\{ xa\ |\ x\in  A \right\} .$$

> [!example]
> ##### 1.) $A=\mathbb{Z}$
> - so $\left\langle a \right\rangle=\left\{ na\ |\ n\in \mathbb{Z} \right\}=\left\{ ...-2a, -a, 0, a, 2a, ... \right\}$
> ##### 2.) $A=\mathbb{Q}$
> - so $\left\langle a \right\rangle=\left\{ \frac{m}{n}a\ |\ \frac{m}{n}\in \mathbb{Q} \right\}$
> 	- $=\left\{ \frac{ma}{n}\ |\ \frac{m}{n}\in \mathbb{Q} \right\}$
> 	- $=\mathbb{Q}.$
> - Since every element $\frac{r}{s}\in\mathbb{Q}$ can be written as $\frac{r}{2s}2\in \left\langle 2 \right\rangle$, since $\frac{r}{2s}\in \mathbb{Q}$.
> - Since $\mathbb{Q}$ is a field, it has no nontrivial proper ideals.
> ##### 3.) $A=\mathcal{P}( S )$
> - where $+$ is symmetric difference and $\cdot$ is intersection
> - Let $a\in A$ s.t. $a\subset S$
> - Then $\left\langle a \right\rangle=\left\{ a\cap x\ |\ x\in \mathcal{P}( S ) \right\}$
> 	- i.e. $x\subset S$

> [!proof] Proof: The principal ideal is an ideal of $A$.
> 1. Clearly, $\left\langle a \right\rangle\subset A$
> 2. Closure under $+$
> 	1. Let $xa,ya\in \left\langle a \right\rangle$
> 	2. Then $xa+ya=( x+y )a\in \left\langle a \right\rangle$.
> 3. Contains $0$
> 	1. $0=0a\in \left\langle a \right\rangle$.
> 4. Closure under negatives
> 	1. Let $xa\in \left\langle a \right\rangle$
> 	2. $-( xa )=( -x )a\in \left\langle a \right\rangle$.
> 5. Absorbs products
> 	1. Let $xa\in \left\langle a \right\rangle$ and $y\in A$
> 	2. $( xa )y=y( xa )$
> 	3. $y( xa )\in \left\langle a \right\rangle$ and $( yx )a\in \left\langle a \right\rangle$.

> [!remark]
> - The ring needs to have unity:
> 	- $a\in \left\langle a \right\rangle$:
> 		- Since $a=1a\in \left\langle a \right\rangle$, $1$ is the unity of $A$
> 	- In fact, $\left\langle a \right\rangle$ is the smallest ideal of $A$ containing $a$.
