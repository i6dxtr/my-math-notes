> [!definition]
> An **ideal** of a [[Rings|ring]] $A$ is a subset $B\subset A$ such that:
> 1. $B$ is a [[Subgroups|subgroup]] of $( A,+ )$
> 2. $B$ "absorbs" products:
> 	1. $\forall x\in B\ \ \forall y\in A, xy\in B$ and $yx\in B$

> [!example]
>
>
> ##### Let $\mathbb{E}$ be the set of even integers.
> 1. $\mathbb{E}$ is an ideal of $\mathbb{Z}$:
> 	1. the sum of two even integers is even
> 	2. the negative of any even integer is even
> 	3. the product of an even integer by *any* integer is always even
>
> ###### Alternatively:
> Let $A=\mathbb{Z}$ and $B=\left\{ 2n\ |\ n\in \mathbb{Z} \right\}$ $=\left\{ ..., -4, -2, 0, 2, 4, ... \right\}$.
> 1. $B$ is an ideal: 
> 	1. It's a subgroup of $( \mathbb{Z},+ )$
> 	2. It absorbs products: $\forall x\in B\ \forall y\in \mathbb{Z},$  $xy\in B$
> 		1. any even integer times another even integer is even
