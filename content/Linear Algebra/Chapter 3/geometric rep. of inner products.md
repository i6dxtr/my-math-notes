- derived from the [[Dot Product|inner product]] spaces:

> [!definition]
> Some $\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{w}\in \mathbb{R}^{n}$ can be geometrically characterized by the equation: $$\mathbf{v}\cdot \mathbf{w}=\lvert \lvert \mathbf{v} \rvert  \rvert \ \lvert \lvert \mathbf{w} \rvert  \rvert \cos \vartheta$$ ... where $\vartheta$ betwn $(\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{w})$ measures the angle between the two vectors

- the absolute value of the product is bounded by the product of the lengths of the vectors:
	- $\lvert \mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{w} \rvert\leq \lvert \lvert \mathbf{v} \rvert \rvert\ \lvert \lvert \mathbf{w} \rvert \rvert$
		- holds if $\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{w}$ are parallel

> [!theorem]
> Every inner product satisfies the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality:
> $$\lvert \left\langle \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{w} \right\rangle \rvert \leq \lvert \lvert \mathbf{v} \rvert  \rvert \ \lvert \lvert \mathbf{w} \rvert  \rvert \text{, }\ \forall \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{w}\in  V.$$
> - $\lvert \lvert \mathbf{v} \rvert \rvert$ associated norm
> - $\lvert \cdot \rvert$ denotes absolute value of a real number

- if two vectors $\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{w}$ have their dot product $\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{w}=0$, then the vectors are [[Orthogonality|orthogonal]]
