### Spectral theorem
*ex.* $A=\begin{pmatrix} 2&0\\0&1 \end{pmatrix}$, restrict $R$ to unit circle $x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}$
1. $R( x )=A\begin{pmatrix} x_{1}\\x_{2} \end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix} x_{1}\\x_{2} \end{pmatrix} / x\cdot x=2x_{2}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}$
2. $R$ at max at $\begin{pmatrix} 1\\0 \end{pmatrix}$, $R\begin{pmatrix} 1\\0 \end{pmatrix}=2$
3. $R$ at min at $\begin{pmatrix} 0\\1 \end{pmatrix}$, $R\begin{pmatrix} 0\\1 \end{pmatrix}=1$
Biggest eigenvalue of $A$ is $2$ where the eigenvector is $\begin{pmatrix} 1\\0 \end{pmatrix}$
Smallest eigenvalue of $A$ is $1$ where the eigenvalue is $\begin{pmatrix} 0\\1 \end{pmatrix}$
Connection to proof of spectral theorem:
- Maximized: $\frac{\lvert\lvert Ax \rvert\rvert}{\lvert\lvert x \rvert\rvert}\rightarrow$ $x^{*}$ max is an eigenvector
- Minimized $\frac{\lvert\lvert A\mathbf{x} \rvert\rvert^{2}}{\lvert\lvert \mathbf{x} \rvert\rvert^{2}}=\frac{A\mathbf{x}\cdot A\mathbf{x}}{\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{x}}=\frac{A^{T}A\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{x}}{\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{x}}$
#### Blunt but useful method
- Take $D=\begin{pmatrix} \lambda_{1}&&\\&\lambda_{2}&\\&&\lambda_{3} \end{pmatrix}$
	- where $\lvert \lambda_{1} \rvert>\lvert \lambda_{2} \rvert\ge \lvert \lambda_{3} \rvert$
- Guess $\mathbf{x}=\begin{pmatrix} x_{1}\\x_{2}\\x_{3} \end{pmatrix}$
- So $\frac{D^{n}\mathbf{x}}{\lvert\lvert D^{n}\mathbf{x} \rvert\rvert}\approx \begin{pmatrix} 1\\\left\lvert  \frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}  \right\rvert^{2}\\\left\lvert  \frac{\lambda_{3}}{\lambda_{1}}  \right\rvert^{2} \end{pmatrix}$, so error is about $\left\lvert  \frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}  \right\rvert^{n}$

### Trefethon/Bau Numerical Linear Algebra
##### Power Iteration
- approximates the largest eigenvalue/vector pair
- random guess (which should have magnitude 1)
	- $A=A^{T}$ is $n \times n$ matrix
	- $V_{0}=$ random magnitude 1 guess
		- x=np.random.rand(n,1) 
		- x=np.linalg.norm(x)
	- for k-1,...,stop
	- Apply matrix $A$
		- $\mathbf{w}=A\mathbf{v}_{k-1}$
		- $\mathbf{v}_{k}=\mathbf{w} / \lvert\lvert \mathbf{w} \rvert\rvert$
		- Apply rally formula
			- $\lambda_{k}=A\mathbf{v}_{k}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{k}( \mathbf{v}_{k}^{T}@ A @ \mathbf{v}_{k} )$
- pretty slow
- also, the gap $\left\lvert  \frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}  \right\rvert$ controls convergence rate
- remark
	- let $A=A^{T}$, - $\mu\in \mathbb{R}$
	- then $A-\mu$ and $A$ have the same eigenvectors:
		- $A-\mu \cdot \mathbb{1}_{n}$
		- b/c $A\mathbf{x}=\lambda\mathbf{x}\rightarrow ( A-\mu )\mathbf{x}=A\mathbf{x}-\mu\mathbf{x}=\lambda\mathbf{x}-\mu \mathbf{x}=( \lambda-\mu )\mathbf{x}$
	- moreover, $B$ and $B^{-1}$ have the same eigenvectors
		- b/c $B\mathbf{x}=\lambda\mathbf{x}\longrightarrow\mathbf{x}=\lambda B^{-1}\mathbf{x}$
			- $=\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathbf{x}$
- idea:
	- for a $\mu$ guess, find eigenvectors for $( A-\mu\mathbb{1}_{n} )^{-1}$
