### Power Iteration
Makes 'stop' many eigenvalues/vector guesses for $A$ to locate the largest eigenvalue/vector pair

==Input==: $A=A^{T}$, scalar $\mu$
- Set $\mathbf{v}=$ magnitude one guess
- for $k=1,2,...,stop$
	- $\mathbf{w}=( A-\mu 1_{n} )^{-1}\mathbf{v}$
		- how can this be done without calculating an inverse?
	- $\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{w} / \lvert\lvert \mathbf{w} \rvert\rvert$
	- $\lambda=R( \mathbf{v} )$
### Inverse Iteration
==Input==: $A=A^{T}$, $\mu$ scalar
- $v_{0}=$ magnitude one guess
- for $k=1,...,$ stop:
	- $W=( A-\mu\mathbb{1}_{n} )^{-1}V_{k-1}$ (use np.linalg.solve($A-\mu\mathbb{1}_{n},V_{k-1}$))
	- $v_{k}=w / \lvert\lvert w \rvert\rvert$
	- $\lambda_{k}=R( V_{k} )$
==Output==: eigenvalue closest to initial guess $\mu$
- Convergence in terms of distance from $\mu$ to eigenvalue

### Rayleigh Quotient Iteration
==Input==: $A=A^{T}, \mu$
- $v_{0}=$ magnitude one guess
- $\lambda_{0}=R(v_{0})$
- for $k=1,...,$ stop:
	- $w=( A-\lambda_{k-1} )^{-1}v_{k-1}$
	- $\lambda_{k}=R( v_{k} )$
==Output==: eigenvalue, not necessarily the largest
