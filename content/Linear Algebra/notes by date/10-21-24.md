### 5.3.1
Given: subspace $H=\text{span}\left\{ \begin{pmatrix} 1\\2\\-1 \end{pmatrix}, \begin{pmatrix} 0\\-1\\3 \end{pmatrix}\right\}$ to $\begin{pmatrix} 1&1&1 \end{pmatrix}$
Find: Orthonormal basis for $H$ ($QR$)
1. $u_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\begin{pmatrix} 1\\2\\1 \end{pmatrix}$
2. $u_{2}=\frac{v_{2}-(v_{i}u_{1})u_{1}}{\lvert \lvert v_{2}-(v_{i}\cdot v_{1})u_{1} \rvert \rvert}$
3. $v_{i}u_{1}=\begin{pmatrix} 0\\-1\\3 \end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix} 1\\2\\-1 \end{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}=\frac{-5}{\sqrt{6}}$
4. $v_{2}-(v_{i}\cdot u_{1})u_{i}=\begin{pmatrix} 0\\-1\\3 \end{pmatrix}+\frac{5}{6}\begin{pmatrix} 1\\2\\-1 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} \frac{5}{6}\\\frac{4}{6}\\ \frac{13}{6} \end{pmatrix}$
5. So $u_{2}=\frac{6}{\sqrt{193}}\begin{pmatrix} \frac{5}{6}\\ \frac{4}{6}\\ \frac{13}{6} \end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{193}}\begin{pmatrix} 5\\4\\13 \end{pmatrix}$
6. Finally, $P\begin{pmatrix} 1\\1\\1 \end{pmatrix}=\left( u_{1}\cdot \begin{pmatrix} 1\\1\\1  \end{pmatrix}\right)u_{1}+\left( u_{2}\cdot\begin{pmatrix} 1\\1\\1 \end{pmatrix}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}u_{1}+\frac{22}{\sqrt{193}}u_{2}=\frac{1}{6}\begin{pmatrix} 1\\2\\-1 \end{pmatrix}+\frac{22}{193}\begin{pmatrix} 5\\4\\13 \end{pmatrix}$
This is showing the Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization process and then using it to find a projection. Let me break down what's happening:

1) First they find $u_1$ by normalizing first vector:
   - $\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$ (since $\sqrt{1^2 + 2^2 + (-1)^2} = \sqrt{6}$)

2) For $u_2$, they take second vector and subtract projection onto $u_1$:
   - Second vector is $v_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$
   - Calculate $v_2 \cdot u_1 = \frac{-5}{\sqrt{6}}$ 
   - Subtract $(v_2 \cdot u_1)u_1$ from $v_2$
   - $v_2 - \frac{-5}{\sqrt{6}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 5/6 \\ 4/6 \\ 13/6 \end{pmatrix}$
   - Normalize result by dividing by $\sqrt{193}$ to get $u_2$

3) Finally, they project vector $\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$ onto this space:
   - Find $(u_1 \cdot \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix})u_1$
   - Find $(u_2 \cdot \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix})u_2$
   - Add these together to get projection

The projection formula $P(v) = (u_1 \cdot v)u_1 + (u_2 \cdot v)u_2$ shows how any vector can be written as a combination of these orthonormal basis vectors.

### 5.6: Convolution
Take $f=\begin{pmatrix} f_{0}\\f_{1}\\\cdots\\f_{n-1} \end{pmatrix}, g=\begin{pmatrix} g_{0}\\g_{1}\\\cdots\\g_{n-1} \end{pmatrix}\in \mathbb{C}^{n}$
1. (not standard) *definition*: $f\not\circ g[k]=\sum_{p=0}^{n-1}f[p]\ g[k-p]$, $0\leq k\leq n-1$
	1. $f\not\circ \mathbb{C}^{n}$
	2. $k-p$ is essentially modulo $n$
==example==. $f=\left\{ \frac{1}{3}, \frac{1}{3}, 0, 0, 0, 0, \frac{1}{3} \right\}\in\mathbb{C}^{7}$, $g=\left\{ 1,2,-1,3,4,2,5 \right\}\in\mathbb{C}^{7}$. 
Calculate $f\not\circ g[ 0]=\sum_{p=0}^{n-1}f[p]\ g[0-p]$:
1. $f[0]g[0]+f[1]g[-1]+f[6]g[-6]$
2. $=$ $f[0]g[0]+f[1]g[6]+f[6]g[1]$
3. $=$ $\frac{1}{3}(g[0]+g[1]+g[6])$
4. $=$ $\frac{1}{3}(8)$
Calculate $f\not\circ g[3]$
1. $f\not\circ g[3]=f[0]g[3]+f[1]g[2]+f[6]g[4]$
2. $=$ $\left\{ \frac{8}{3}, ..., ..., 2, ..., ... \right\}$
3. $=$ $\left\{ \frac{8}{3}, \frac{2}{3}, \frac{4}{3},2, 3, \frac{11}{3} \right\}$
... so convolution is a ID-version of Schur-sum.
==remark==. 
- $f\not\circ (g+h)=f \not\circ g+f\not\circ h$
- $f\not\circ (\alpha g)=\alpha(f\not\circ g)$ where $\alpha$ is a scalar

Rewriting above formulas as matrix multiplication yields the following: $$\begin{pmatrix} f_{1}&f_{1}&f_{2}&f_{3}&\cdots&f_{n-1}\\f_{n-1}&f_{0}&f_{n}\cdots &\cdots &\cdots & \\ \cdots \\f_{3} \\f_{2} &f_{3} & & & & f_{1} \\f_{1} & f_{2} & f_{3} & & f_{n-1}& f_{0} \end{pmatrix}@\begin{pmatrix} g_{0}\\g_{1}\\\cdots\\g_{n-1} \end{pmatrix}=f\not\circ g$$
==Key Idea==. The Fourier transform $\frac{1}{n}\overline{F}n$ diagonalizes $C_{f}$ *for all values* of $f$.
$$F_{n}^{-1}C_{f}F_{n}=\begin{pmatrix} \frac{1}{n}\overline{F}_{n}[f][0]&\cdots& \cdots\\\cdots & \frac{1}{n}\overline{F}_{n}[f][1]& \cdots \\\cdots & \cdots & \frac{1}{n}\overline{F}_{n}[f][n-1] \end{pmatrix}$$
==Theorem==. $\text{DFT}(f\not\circ g)=\text{DFT}(f)*\text{DFT}(g)$
- LHS takes $n^{2}$ operations
- RHS takes $n$ operations
*why?* 
1. $\text{DFT}(f\not\circ g)=F_{n}^{-1}(f\not\circ g)$ 
2. $=$ $F_{n}^{-1}(fg)$ 
3. $=$ $F_{n}^{-1}(fF_{n}F_{n}^{-1}g)$
4. $=$ $\text{DFT}(f)*\text{DFT}(g)$
