> [!theorem]
> $f:E\rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is continuous *if and only if* for any open $\vartheta$ subset of $\mathbb{R},$ $f^{-1}( \vartheta )$ is open in $E.$
> *"There exists an open $U$ such that $f^{-1}( \vartheta )=U\cap E$."*

> [!remark]
> Every open subset of $\mathbb{R}$ is a countable union of open intervals. $$\vartheta=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}I_{n}$$... implying $f:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}$ is continuous if and only if for every open interval $I$ in $\mathbb{R}$, $f^{-1}( I )$ is open in $E$.

> [!theorem]
> If $K$ is a compact subset of $\mathbb{R}$ and $f:K\rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ where $f$ is continuous, then $f( K )$ is compact.

> [!proof]
> - Let $( \vartheta_{\alpha} )_{\alpha\in I}$ be an open cover of $f( K ).$
> - Since $f$ is continuous, $f^{-1}( O_{\alpha} )$ is open in $K$. 
> - There exists $U_{\alpha}$ that is open such that $f^{-1}( O_{\alpha})=U_{\alpha} \cap K$
> - $( U_{\alpha} )_{\alpha\in I}$ is an open cover of $K$.
> 	- $K=\bigcup_{\alpha}^{}f^{-1}( O_{\alpha} )=f^{-1}\left(  \bigcup_{\alpha\in I}^{}O_{\alpha}  \right)$
> 	- $x\in K, f( x )\in f( K )\longrightarrow \exists \alpha_{0}, f( x )\in O_{\alpha_{0}}$ etc
> - Since $K$ is compact, $\exists \alpha_{1}, \alpha_{2}, \cdots, \alpha_{N}$ such that $K\subset U_{\alpha_{1}}\cup U_{\alpha_{2}}\cup\cdots\cup U_{\alpha_{N}}.$
> - We will verify that $f( K )\subseteq O_{\alpha_{1}}\cup O_{\alpha_{2}}\cup\cdots\cup O_{\alpha_{N}}$
> 	- Let $y\in f( K )$
> 	- Then $\exists x\in K$ such that $y=f( x )$
> 	- So $\exists j\in \left\{ 1,2,..., N \right\}$ such that $x\in U_{\alpha_{j}}$
> 	- Moreover, $x\in U_{\alpha_{j}}\cap K = f^{-1}( O_{\alpha_{j}} )$
> 	- Therefore $f( x )\in O_{\alpha_{j}}.$

> [!example]
> $$f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow \mathbb{R}, x\rightarrow x^{2}$$
> 1. $f( \mathbb{R} )=[0, \infty)$ is *not* compact
> 2. $g:(0,1]\rightarrow \mathbb{R}, x\rightarrow \frac{1}{x}$
> 	1. Continuous
> 	2. $g( (0,1] )=[1, \infty)$ is closed, but *not* compact
> 3. $h:[a, 1]\rightarrow \mathbb{R}, x\rightarrow \frac{1}{x}$ where $0<a<1$
> 	1. $h( \left[ a, 1 \right] )=[1, \frac{1}{a}]$

> [!corollary]
> Let $K$ be a compact subset of $\mathbb{R}$ and $f:K\rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ where $f$ is continuous. Then, there exists $p, q\in K$ such that $f( p )\le f( x )\le f( p ),$ for all $x\in K.$

> [!proof]
> - Observe that $f( K )$ is compact.
> - In particular, it is bounded.
> - Let $M=\text{sup}\left\{ f( x ):x\in K \right\}$
> - If $M\notin f( K )$
> 	- $M$ is a limit point of $f( K )$
> 	- So $\exists ( y_{n} )\subseteq f( K )$ such that $y_{n}\rightarrow M$.
> 	- So $\forall n\ge 1, y_{n}=f( x_{n} )$ for $x_{n}\in K$
> 	- So $( x_{n} )\subset K$ (compact)
> 	- Therefore there exists a subsequence $x_{n_{k}}\rightarrow x\in K$
> 	- By continuity, $f( x_{n_{k}} )\rightarrow f( x )$
> 	- In other words, $f( x_{n_{k}} )\rightarrow f( x )$
> 	- Which implies $M=f( x )\in f( K )$, a contradiction
#### Intermediate Value Theorem

> [!definition]
> Let $f:\left[ a, b \right]\rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be a continuous function. Suppose $f( a )<f( b ).$ If $f( a )<\gamma<f( b )$, then $\exists c\in \left[ a,b \right]$ such that $f( c )=\gamma.$ $$\begin{gathered} f( a )<\gamma <f( b ) \Longrightarrow f( c )=\gamma \\ \text{for some }c\in  \left[ a,b \right] \end{gathered}$$

> [!proof]
> - Fix $f( a )<\gamma<f( b ).$
> - Let $A=\left\{ x\in \left[ a,b \right]:f( x )\le \gamma \right\}.$
> - Assume $A\ne \emptyset$ (since $a\in A$)
> - Moreover, $A$ is bounded.
> - Let $c=\text{sup }A.$
> - ==Remark==: $A=f^{-1}( (\infty, \gamma ])$ is closed in $\left[ a, b \right]$
> 	- $A= f^{-1}( \mathbb{R} - ( \alpha, \infty ) )$
> 	- $\ \ \ = \left[ a,b \right]- f^{-1}( ( \alpha, \infty ) )$
> - $A$ is a complement of an open subset of $\left[ a,b \right]$
> - This implies $A$ is a closed subset of $\left[ a, b \right]$
> 	- meaning $A$ is compact $\Longrightarrow$ $c\in A$
> - So $f( c )\le \gamma$
> ---
> - Assume $f( c )<\gamma$
> - So $\gamma-f( c )>0$
> - Fix $\varepsilon>0$ 
> - So $\exists \delta>0$ such that if $\lvert x-c \rvert<\delta$ and $x\in \left[ a, b \right]$ then $\lvert f( x )-f( c ) \rvert<\varepsilon.$
> - Suppose $\varepsilon=\gamma-f( c )>0$
> - Then $f( c )+\varepsilon=\gamma$
> - Observe: $x\in( c, c+\delta )\longrightarrow f( x )<\gamma$
> 	- Contradiction: $x>c, c=\text{sup }A$
> - Thus, $f( c )=\gamma.$
