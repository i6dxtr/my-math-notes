### Section 1.4 #9  
*Let $E$ be a nonempty subset of $\mathbb{R}$ that is bounded above, and set*
$$
U = \{\beta \in \mathbb{R} : \beta \text{ is an upper bound of } E\}.
$$
*Prove that $\sup E = \inf U$.*
#### Solution
**Given**
- $E$ is bounded
- Set $U=\left\{ \beta\in \mathbb{R} : \beta \text{ is an upper bound of }E. \right\}$
- Let $A=\text{sup }E$
- Let $B=\text{Inf }U$
**Proof**
- Since $A$ is an upper bound of $E$, $A\in U$
	- So $\text{Inf }U\le A$
- If $\beta \in U$, $\beta$ is an upper bound of $E$.
- By definition of $A$, $A\le \beta$
- So $A$ is a lower bound of $U$
	- Implying $A\le \text{Inf }U$

*Let $\left\{ a_{n} \right\}$ be a sequence of positive real numbers such that:* $$\lim_{n \to \infty}\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_{n}}=L.$$ *If $L<1$, prove that the sequence $\left\{ a_{n} \right\}$ converges to zero.*
##### Solution
- $\exists n_{0}\in \mathbb{N}$, $n\ge n_{0}$ where $\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_{n}}\in ( L-\varepsilon, L+ \varepsilon )$
- $\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_{n}}<L+\varepsilon \longrightarrow a_{n+1}<( l+\varepsilon )a_{n}$
- $a_{n+1}<( L+\varepsilon )a_{n}<( L+\varepsilon )^{2}a_{n-1}<\cdots<( L+\varepsilon )^{}a_{n_{0}}$
- $a_{n+1}<( L+\varepsilon )^{n-n_{0}+1}a_{n_{0}}$
- $a_{n}<( L+\varepsilon )^{n-n_{0}}a_{n_{0}}$  $\forall n>n_{0}$
- $a_{n}<( L+\varepsilon )^{n}( L+\varepsilon )^{n-n_{0}}a_{n_{0}}$
	- $( L+\varepsilon )^{n}$ a sequence
	- $( L+\varepsilon )^{n-n_{0}}a_{n_{0}}$ a fixed number
	- $( L+\varepsilon )^{n}\longrightarrow 0$ since $L+\varepsilon<1$
