#### 3.4 q7
> [!example]
> Let $( p_{n} )$ be a bounded sequence and $p\in \mathbb{R}$ such that every convergent subsequence of $\left\{ p_{n} \right\}$ converges to $p$. Prove that $p_{n}\longrightarrow p.$
> [!proof]
> Suppose $p_{n}\longrightarrow p$. Then $\exists \varepsilon>0$ such that $\forall n\in \mathbb{N}, \exists k\ge n$ such that $\lvert p_{k}-p \rvert\ge \varepsilon.$
> *Claim:* $\exists$ a subsequence $( p_{n_{k}} )$ with $\lvert p_{n_{k}}-p\rvert\ge \varepsilon$ for all $k\ge 1$
> *By induction.*
> - Where $n=1,$
> 	- $\exists n_{1}\ge 1$ such that $\lvert p_{n_{1}}-p \rvert\ge \varepsilon.$
> 	- Assume that $\exists n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots<n_{k}$ with $\lvert p_{n_{j}}-p \rvert\ge \varepsilon$ for $j\in \left\{ 1,2,\cdots,k \right\}.$
> - Where $n=n_{k}+1$
> 	- $\exists n_{k+1}\ge n_{k}+1$ such that $\lvert p_{n_{k+1}}-p \rvert\ge \varepsilon$
> - Since $\left\{ p_{n_{k}} \right\}$ is bounded, by Bolzano-Weirstrass, there exists a subsequence $\left\{ p_{n_{k_{j}}} \right\}$ of $\left\{ p_{_{n_{k}}} \right\}$ that is convergent. 
> - By assumption, $p_{n_{k_{j}}}\longrightarrow p$.
> 	- Contradiction: since $\lvert p_{n_{k_{j}}}-p \rvert\ge \varepsilon.$
#### 3.5 q7
> [!example]
> If $( a_{n} )$ and $( b_{n} )$ are positive sequences, prove that $\overline{\lim_{  }}( a_{n}b_{n} )\le \overline{\lim_{  }}b_{n}.$
> [!proof]
> - Suppose $L=\overline{\lim_{n \to \infty}}a_{n}$ and $s=\overline{\lim_{  }}b_{n}.$
> - Let $L=\lim_{k \to \infty}\alpha_{k}$ where $a_{k}=\text{sup}\left\{ a_{n}n\ge k \right\}$
> - Let $s=\lim_{k \to \infty}\beta_{k}$ where $\beta_{k}=\text{sup}\left\{ b_{n}, n\ge k \right\}$
> - Observe: $a_{n}b_{n}\le \alpha_{k}\beta_{k}$
> 	- $n\ge k$
> - Let $A_{k}=\left\{ a_{n}b_{n}, n\ge k \right\}$
> - Then $\alpha_{k}\beta_{k}$ is an upper bound of $A_{k}$
> 	- $\text{sup}\left\{ a_{n}b_{n}, n\ge k \right\}\le \alpha_{k}\beta_{k}$
> 	- so $\lim_{k \to \infty}\text{sup}\left\{ a_{n}b_{n}, n\ge k \right\}\le \lim_{k \to \infty}\alpha_{k}\beta_{k}$
> 		- equivalent to $( \lim_{k \to \infty}\alpha_{k} )( \lim_{k \to \infty}\beta_{k} )$
> 		- which is $\overline{\lim_{n \to \infty }}a_{n}b_{n}$
> Example: $a_{n}=\begin{cases} 1&n\text{ is odd} \\ 2&n\text{ is even} \end{cases}$
> - $a_{n}b_{n}=2$
> - $\lim_{n \to \infty}( a_{n}b_{n} )=2$
> 	- $\overline{\lim_{  }}a_{n}=2$
> 	- $\varliminf_{  }b_{n}=2$
#### 3.6 q4
> [!example]
> Given $$s_{n}=1+\frac{1}{2}+\cdots+\frac{1}{n}$$... prove $\left\{ s_{n} \right\}$ is *not* a [[Cauchy sequences|Cauchy sequence]].
> [!proof]
> - If $s_{n}$ is not cauchy, then $\exists \varepsilon>0$ such that $\forall N, \exists n,m\ge N$ with $\lvert x_{n}-x_{m} \rvert\ge \varepsilon.$
> - Take $s_{n}-s_{m}=\frac{1}{m+1}+\frac{1}{m+2}+\cdots+\frac{1}{n}$ where $n>m$
> 	- $\ge \frac{1}{n}$ (# term) 
> 	- $=\frac{n-m}{n}$
> - where $n=2m$
> 	- $s_{2m}-s_{m}\ge \frac{2m-n}{2m}=\frac{1}{2}$
> - take $\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2}$
> 	- so $\forall m, \lvert s_{2m}-s_{m} \rvert\ge \frac{1}{2},$ a contradiction
> (b.) let $\lim_{n \to \infty}\lvert s_{n+k}-s_{n} \rvert=0$ for all $k\ge 1$
> - $s_{n+k}-s_{n}=\frac{1}{n+1}+\frac{1}{n+2}+\cdots+\frac{1}{n+k}$
> 	- each term tends to 0
