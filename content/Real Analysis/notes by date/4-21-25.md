#### Sequences and series of functions
> [!definition]
> A **sequence of functions** is defined $f_{n}:E\rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ where $n\ge 1,$ written as $( f_{n} )_{n\ge 1}.$

> [!example]
> Define $f_{n}:\left[ 0,1 \right]\rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ where $f_{n}( x )=x^{n}.$ Observe:
> - $f_{1}=x$
> - $f_{2}=x^{2}$
> - $f_{3}=x^{4}$
> - ...
> ... is a series of functions.
#### Pointwise convergence / pointwise limit
> [!definition]
> Let $f_{n}:E\rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ be a sequence of functions. We say that $( f_{n} )_{n\ge 1}$ **converges to $f:E\rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ pointwise** if $\forall x\in E,$ $( f_{n}( x ) )_{n\ge 1}$ converges to $f( x )$ in $\mathbb{R}.$ In this case, $f$ is called a **pointwise limit** of $( f_{n} )_{n\ge 1}.$
#### Uniform convergence
> [!definition]
> Let $f_{n}:E\rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ and $f:E\rightarrow \mathbb{R}.$ We say $( f_{n} )_{n\ge 1}$ converges to $f$ **uniformly on $E$** if $\forall \varepsilon>0, \exists n_{0}$ such that $\forall n\ge n_{0},$ $\lvert f_{n}( x )-f( x ) \rvert<\varepsilon$ for all $x\in E.$
